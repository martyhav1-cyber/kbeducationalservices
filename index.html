A child-centered approach to learning

With more than 35 years of experience working with children and adolescents, I bring the perspective of an educator, school counselor, school psychologist, learning specialist, executive functioning coach, and academic coach to each student I support.

Effective tutoring is not simply about repeating what was taught in the classroom. It is about understanding the individual child, identifying what is not clicking, and finding an approach that does.

I design instruction around each student's learning needs, strengths, confidence, motivation, and academic goals so children can experience success while developing greater independence.

My goal:
Help each child understand how they learn, discover strategies that work for them, and build the confidence to use those strategies independently.

How I can help

Support is personalized to the child rather than delivered as a one-size-fits-all program.

Early Literacy & Reading Readiness

Foundational literacy skills, sound awareness, letter-sound learning, early reading readiness, and confidence with beginning academic tasks.

K–5 Academic & Homework Support

Guided support across school subjects, with emphasis on understanding directions, organizing work, completing assignments, and building independence.

Writing Foundations

Support with written expression, sentence and paragraph development, organization, revision, editing, and putting ideas into clear written form.

Executive Functioning

Practical strategies for task initiation, organization, time management, planning, follow-through, and study habits.

Metacognitive Learning Skills

Teaching children to understand how they learn, recognize which strategies work best, monitor their progress, and become more effective problem solvers.

Support for Unique Learners

Individualized instruction for neurodivergent students and children who benefit from a more flexible approach, including learners with ADHD and dyslexia.

More than academic tutoring

Academic skills matter, but so do confidence, motivation, self-awareness, and the ability to approach learning independently.

Understand the learner

I look at the whole child — strengths, learning style, frustration points, confidence, and educational needs.

Find what clicks

When a classroom approach is not working, I adjust the presentation, pacing, supports, and strategies to make learning more accessible.

Build learning awareness

Students are taught to notice what helps them learn, remember, organize, and solve problems — the foundation of metacognition.

Grow independence

The long-term goal is not dependence on a tutor. It is helping students develop tools they can eventually use on their own.
